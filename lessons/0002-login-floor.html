Lesson 02 · RunMax API · Auth

Your login already hashes. That is not the floor.

You asked whether POST /login meets the durable minimum. Hashing is the part that does. The skill is smaller than the checklist: tell a floor rule from a newer preference, then mark your own verifier.

The win

You can put a control from apps/api/src/modules/auth/route.ts into one of three bins — floor fail, floor pass, not the floor — and you will not add MFA just to “meet the standard.”

1 · The filter

A control is on the floor only if all three answers are yes. Your login handler is the verifier: the code that checks the password and issues the session secret.

  1. Has the requirement survived, not just the latest number? A rule that has held across revisions is Lindy. A number that moved last year is not.
  2. Is it required at the lowest level — a NIST SHALL, or OWASP ASVS Level 1 — rather than a SHOULD or a Level 3 control?
  3. Does this handler actually do it? A TODO is not a pass.
Lindy, applied here: the longer a requirement has held, the less likely it is a fashion. Salt plus a cost factor has been required since at least NIST SP 800-63B §5.1.1.2 (June 2017). Rate-limiting failed attempts on the account is a SHALL in that same section, and still a SHALL in 800-63B-4 (current 1 Aug 2025).

One number that did not survive: subscriber-chosen passwords SHALL be at least 8 characters in the 2017 text. The 2025 text raises single-factor passwords to 15. The durable rule is “enforce a minimum, plus a blocklist, and do not demand uppercase-digit-symbol.” The 15 is current NIST. It is not old enough to be the floor. Enforce length on register, not inside login.

2 · Three verdicts, from your code

Floor pass — argon2.verify

verifyPassword calls argon2.verify, and hashingPassword calls argon2.hash with no weakened options. The requirement is a salt and a cost factor, not the brand name Argon2. node-argon2 says its defaults follow the Argon2 team and should not be changed for passwords. The hash is not copied into the JWT. That part stands.

Floor fail — the password is in the log

Line 47 of route.ts is console.log({ email, password }). signToken then logs { user, secret }, and the object you pass includes hash. The route also logs signedToken. The OWASP Logging Cheat Sheet says not to record authentication passwords, access tokens, session identifiers, or encryption keys. A log drain is then a credential store. This one miss is enough to fail the floor.

Not the floor — MFA

Multi-factor is NIST Authenticator Assurance Level 2. In OWASP ASVS 4.0.3, impersonation resistance (V2.2.4) is Level 3 only. It is the right next step if an account can spend money or expose someone else’s data. It is not the minimum for this login.

Same words, different time

Returning one sentence is not enough if a missing user skips Argon2 and a wrong password runs it. The OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet shows that early return as a timing enumeration: the attacker learns the account exists from how long you take. Your handler does both — "Invalid email" before verify, "Invalid password" after.

3 · Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. The options are the same length on purpose.

Q1 · NIST raised the single-factor minimum from 8 (2017) to 15 (2025). Is 15 on the floor?

  • Correct. The requirement that survived is “a minimum, plus a blocklist, no composition rules.” The digit 15 is one revision old.

  • No. Current text is what you implement when you choose to track NIST. It is not automatically the durable floor.

  • No. Longer is often better. “Always safest” is not the test. The test is whether the requirement has held.

  • No. Composition rules (uppercase, digit, symbol) are what NIST tells you not to impose. A minimum length is still required.

Q2 · Missing user returns the same 401 text, but skips Argon2. Verdict?

  • Correct. Enumeration is any difference the attacker can measure. Time is a difference. Run a dummy verify on the miss.

  • No. Identical JSON does not hide a handler that returns before the slow hash.

  • No. The cheat sheet treats the early return as the bug, not as an optional extra.

  • No. In this question the status is already the same 401. The remaining leak is duration, not the code.

Q3 · Opaque server session, or a rotating refresh token. Which meets the floor?

  • Correct. The old requirement is a session secret you can expire and kill. Rotation is one shape. A row you delete is the other.

  • No. Refresh rotation is your TODO, and it is fine. It is not more standard than a server-side session.

  • No. A JWT nothing can revoke fails the floor. verifyToken is empty, so yours cannot even be checked.

  • No. MFA is the next level, not the condition for a revocable session.
